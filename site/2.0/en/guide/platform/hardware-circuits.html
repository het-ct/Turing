Standard Circuits and Review Checklist

The platform hardware is organized into four modules: power supply, load, communication and HMI. When reusing a proven reference circuit, it still needs to be recalculated for the target device, PCB stackup, structure, and regulatory requirements; you should not just copy the schematic network.

power circuit

Covers AC-DC, LDO, BUCK, BOOST, battery, charging, power path and filtering. Input protection, derating, loop stability, ripple, power-up timing, standby power consumption, thermal paths, and test points are checked during review.

load circuit

  • Motor/Fan/Pump/Valve: Calculate starting and stalling currents, and add freewheeling, overcurrent, overtemperature and reverse connection protection.

  • Heating/Cooling: Isolate control and power loops, deal with temperature sensor failure and relay/power tube adhesion.

  • Lighting & Backlighting: Using constant current or dedicated drivers, verify PWM frequency, EMI, low-light flicker and thermal decay.

Communication and storage

Standard modules include RS-485, CAN, USB, Ethernet, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, Flash and SD cards, and more. The differential interface maintains continuous impedance, and ESD protection is arranged on the connector side; the crystal oscillator, Flash and high-speed clock traces are short and have a complete reference ground.

HMI circuit

TFT, Touch, PEDOT, audio and external storage often share power and GPIO multiplexing resources. Before freezing the schematic, it should be confirmed with the software:

  1. Interface bandwidth can cover target resolutions, refresh rates, and pixel formats.

  2. Reset, backlight, TE, touch interrupt and audio mute pin all have certain default states.

  3. There are no conflicts in the reuse groups of display, camera, SD and external Flash.

  4. Analog audio, RF, and switching power supply layout areas are isolated from each other with continuous return paths.

design deliverables

  • Schematic, PCB, BOM, Gerber, coordinate and controlled footprint libraries.

  • Power budget, thermal analysis, critical timing and interface bandwidth calculations.

  • Board-level startup checklist, test point definition, production test plan and fault injection records.

  • Pin tables, screen/touch configurations and key material AVLs tied to software engineering builds.

For more information on chip boundaries and packaging see Electrical Characteristics and Design Boundaries and Packaging and development board。
